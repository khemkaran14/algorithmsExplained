# Distributed Cache Design

## Hook

Place keys on distinct physical cache replicas. A useful design makes ownership, trade-offs and failure outcomes explicit.

## Watch

Build the architecture one component at a time. Read the requirements and estimates above the player, then inspect the modeled state transitions.

## How it works

The origin owns durable versions while cache entries may expire or be evicted.

Virtual tokens determine owners but are not physical replication.

Read healthy owners in ring order, then fill from the origin on a miss.

Write-through updates healthy owners and may leave a disconnected owner stale.

A recovered stale replica requires invalidation, version validation or bounded TTL.

## Try it

Predict an input-dependent state transition before advancing. Change one assumption at a time and account for committed, rejected, stale or pending outcomes.

### Break it

Warm a key, fail its primary, write a new value, then restore the primary before TTL. Inspect the freshness audit; disable the origin to distinguish replica hits from failed misses.

## When to use

Use the exercise to explain requirements, ownership, scaling pressure and failure policy in a design interview. Validate a production design with actual workload measurements and the complete selected provider protocol.

## Interview pitfalls

Do not turn an illustrative throughput parameter into a universal benchmark. Distinguish retry safety from exactly-once external effects, a cache from durable ownership, and a timeout from a known failure. Name the model limits before claiming a guarantee.

## Quiz

Explain one state transition, one failure invariant and one concern outside the bounded simulation.
