# Euclidean Gcd

## Hook

Cut two ribbons into equal pieces; removing whole copies of the shorter ribbon does not change the largest shared piece size.

## Watch

## How it works

1. Compute a mod b.
2. Replace a with b and b with the remainder.
3. Stop when b is zero.

## Complexity

Each Euclidean remainder reduces the positive operands quickly; consecutive Fibonacci numbers give the long-run logarithmic bound. The algorithm convention here returns zero for gcd(0,0). The chart names its counted or modeled work rather than pretending animation duration is a benchmark.

## When to use

Use it for simplifying fractions, modular arithmetic and divisibility reasoning.

## Interview pitfalls

Avoid division by zero by testing b first. The demonstration accepts nonnegative integers; signed minimum values need care when taking absolute values.
